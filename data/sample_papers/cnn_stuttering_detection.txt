Speaker-Independent Stuttering Detection with Convolutional Neural Networks: A Summary

Stuttering is a speech disorder marked by repetitions, prolongations and blocks. Automatic detection can support speech-language pathologists by quantifying disfluencies in long recordings. A central challenge is speaker independence: a model trained on some speakers must generalise to speakers it has never heard.

The approach converts audio into log-mel spectrograms and MFCC features, which are fed to a convolutional neural network. Convolutional layers learn local time-frequency patterns, such as the repeated onsets seen in sound repetitions. Pooling layers reduce resolution and a dense classifier predicts the disfluency type for each short window.

To measure speaker independence, evaluation uses speaker-disjoint splits so that no speaker appears in both training and test data. Random window-level splits leak speaker identity and report inflated accuracy. Reported metrics include per-class F1 score, because disfluency classes are imbalanced and fluent speech dominates.

Data augmentation such as time stretching, pitch shifting and added noise improves robustness. Class weighting and focal loss help with the imbalance between fluent and disfluent segments.
